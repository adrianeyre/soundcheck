Make a lo-fi hip-hop loop: 8 bars unless the musician says otherwise.

1. Set the tempo the musician gives, or 85 BPM (70 to 90). Use their key, or D minor, with jazzy chords such as ii7–V7–Imaj7 or im9–ivm9, one to a bar.
2. Drums, boom-bap: the Kick on 1 and the "and" of 2 (and sometimes 3's "and"); the Snare on 2 and 4 at about 100; a Closed Hat on eighths, heavily swung; a few ghost snares at about 40. Humanise it loosely as /humanise does, with the snare slightly late for a lazy feel.
3. Keys: Electric Keys (or Tremolo Keys for a wobbling, tape-like sound), playing the chords in five-note voicings, with the notes of each chord spread a little as a pianist's would be, humanised.
4. Bass: Sub Bass, round and quiet, on the root of each chord with a note leading into the next.
5. Melody, optional: Bell Pluck playing a few sparse notes over the chords.
6. Make it dusty: on the keys and the Master, add an EQ with its low cut at about 60 Hz and its high cut on at about 7 kHz. Send the keys to a Reverb Bus with a small, dark room (size 0.3, damping 0.8). Keep the Master quieter than for a club track: about -14 LUFS or below.

Say the tempo, key, chords, and what each Track plays.
