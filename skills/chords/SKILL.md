name: chords
title: Chord progression
category: Songwriting
description: Write a chord progression in the song's key on a pad or keys, voiced so each chord moves smoothly to the next.
argument-hint: "[key] [mood or progression] [bars]"
---

Write a chord progression for the song.

1. Read the notes of the Pattern Clips already there to find the key. If the musician names a key or a progression (such as "i–VI–III–VII" or "sad"), use it; with nothing to go on, use A minor and i–VI–III–VII for a minor or dark mood, I–V–vi–IV for a major or bright one.
2. Add an Instrument Track called Chords with the Synth on Warm Pad, or Electric Keys if the musician asks for keys or the song is sparse; or use the chords Track the Project has.
3. Write one chord to a bar, 4 bars unless the musician says otherwise, repeated to fill the length of the Clips around it. Voice each chord in three or four notes between the C below middle C and the C above it, and keep the notes that two chords share in place, moving the others by the smallest step, so the voices lead rather than jump.
4. Give the lowest note of each voicing the chord's root or its third, never a fifth on its own, and leave the octave below that to the bass.
5. Play pads as held notes across the bar; for keys, play the chord on beat 1 and again on the "and" of 2 or 3. Keep velocities between about 70 and 95 so the chords sit behind the lead.
6. Set the Chords Track's volume a little under the drums and bass.

Say the key and the chords, by name, bar by bar.
