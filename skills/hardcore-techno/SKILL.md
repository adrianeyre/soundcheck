Make a hardcore techno loop: 8 bars unless the musician says otherwise, at the top of the song or after the last Clip. Use the Tracks the Project already has for drums, bass and lead rather than adding more.

1. Set the tempo the musician gives, or 170 BPM. Use their key, or F minor; hardcore sits in minor keys, often leaning on the flat second (Phrygian) for menace.
2. Drums, on a Drum Track with a Drum Sampler: the Kick on every beat at velocity 127, as the whole track rests on it. Closed Hat on every off-beat eighth, and on sixteenths in the last bar of every 4; a Clap on beats 2 and 4 at about 90, low in the mix, or none. No swing: hardcore is rigid.
3. The kick must be hard. If one of the musician's saved Kits has a hardcore or distorted kick, load it. If a distortion or bitcrusher Plugin is installed, add it to the Drum Track's Insert Chain. Then add an EQ with low shelf about +4 dB at about 60 Hz and band 2 about +3 dB at about 2.5 kHz for the click, then a Compressor at ratio about 8:1, attack 1 ms, release 60 ms, with makeup gain. Without a distorting Plugin or Kit, say that the kick can't be distorted with the built-in Effects alone.
4. Bass: an Instrument Track called Rumble with the Synth on Reese Bass, playing the root on each off-beat eighth between kicks, an octave below the C below middle C. Duck it under each kick as /pump does, heavily. Cut the Drum Track's EQ band 1 about 3 dB where the bass sits, if the two fight.
5. Stabs: an Instrument Track called Stabs with Saw Lead, playing short minor chords (a sixteenth or two long) on a syncopated rhythm, such as the "and" of 1 and the "e" of 3, varying every 2 bars. Send it to a Reverb Bus with a short, dark reverb (decay about 1.2 s, damping 0.8), making one as /reverb-bus does if there is none.
6. Keep the mix's true peak under the ceiling; turn down the stabs before the kick.

Say the tempo, key, and what each Track plays.
